Audio and lip sync

The phone's microphone has two possible jobs, and it can only do one of them at a time: it can be the audio you hear, or it can be a timing reference used to align the microphone you actually use. Turning one on turns the other off.

The phone as a microphone

Turn on Send phone mic to OBS in the app's Options and the camera source carries the phone's microphone as its audio — the phone doubles as a wireless mic. It is off by default, because most streamers use their own microphone and line it up instead.

A mic row on the Live screen and in the browser panel picks which microphone:

ChoiceWhat you get
AutoiOS's default input routing — normally the Bottom mic, regardless of which camera is active, since iOS does not tie the mic to the camera. A connected headset or Bluetooth mic takes over automatically.
FrontThe front-facing mic array, aimed roughly where the front camera looks.
BackThe rear mic, aimed at whatever the rear camera sees.

The switch is live — no restart, no dropped stream.

Automatic lip sync

Any camera over any link arrives a little late. If your voice comes from a separate microphone, that microphone is early relative to the picture, and you get the classic dubbed-film effect. The usual fix is to guess a delay value in OBS and nudge it until it looks right. LensLink measures it instead.

Setting it up

  1. In the LensLink Camera source properties

    Pick your real microphone under Lip-sync audio source.

    Enable Automatically delay that audio source to match the camera latency, and enable Auto-calibrate.

  2. In the app's Options

    Turn on Auto lip-sync reference. The phone now sends its microphone purely as a timing signal — it is never played, never recorded, and never reaches your stream.

  3. Talk for about fifteen seconds

    The plugin compares the two audio streams, measures the exact offset, and corrects it. The Live screen and the browser panel show the stage: Measuring sync in blue, then Sync locked in green.

Once it is locked

Your microphone's own delay is now a known quantity, so the correction keeps following the camera's latency by itself — through silence, and straight after a reconnect — instead of waiting for you to speak again. It re-checks periodically and recalibrates on its own if you change audio gear mid-stream.

To force a fresh measurement, use Recalibrate: tap the sync pill on the phone's Live screen, or press the small button inside the pill in the browser panel. Both are only offered while locked, because that is the one state where recalibrating does anything.

ReadoutMeaning
Nothing shownAuto-calibrate is not running. Nothing to learn, nothing to ignore.
Measuring syncWorking on it. Keep talking.
Sync lockedDone. The offset is applied and tracked.
RecalibratingRe-measuring after a change — transitional, not an error.

When your microphone is the slow one

Some USB audio interfaces add enough latency of their own that the microphone arrives after the video. Delaying it further cannot help. Enable Auto video delay for slow mics and the plugin delays the video to match instead.

A fixed correction, without auto-calibrate

Leave Auto-calibrate off and the delay checkbox alone shifts your microphone by the measured camera latency. If your audio device adds a known delay of its own, enter it under Audio device's own latency and it is subtracted from the correction.

Why not just set a number in OBS? Because the camera's latency is not a constant — it moves with resolution, codec, link type and network conditions. A measured offset that tracks those changes stays right after you switch from 1080p60 Wi-Fi to 4K30 USB; a typed one does not.

Screen-mirror audio

The LensLink Screen source carries the phone's app and system audio, which is genuine, playable audio — not a timing reference. iOS mutes DRM-protected apps during any broadcast, and your microphone is not included. Details are on the screen mirroring page.
