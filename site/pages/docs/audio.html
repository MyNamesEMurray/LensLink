Audio and lip sync

The phone's microphone has two possible jobs and can only do one: be the audio you hear, or be a timing reference that aligns the microphone you actually use. Turning one on turns the other off.

The phone as a microphone

Options → Send phone mic to OBS makes the camera source carry the phone's microphone — a wireless mic, in effect. Off by default, since most streamers use their own mic and align it instead.

A mic row on the Live screen and in the browser panel picks which one:

ChoiceWhat you get
AutoiOS's default routing — normally the Bottom mic whatever camera is active, since iOS doesn't tie the two together. A headset or Bluetooth mic takes over automatically.
FrontThe front array, aimed roughly where the front camera looks.
BackThe rear mic, aimed at what the rear camera sees.

The switch is live.

Automatic lip sync

Video always arrives a little late, so a separate microphone runs early against it — the dubbed-film effect. The usual fix is to guess a delay in OBS and nudge it. LensLink measures it instead.

Setting it up

  1. In the source properties

    Pick your real microphone under Lip-sync audio source, then enable Automatically delay that audio source… and Auto-calibrate.

  2. In the app's Options

    Turn on Auto lip-sync reference. The phone's mic is now a timing signal only — never played, never recorded, never on your stream.

  3. Talk for about fifteen seconds

    The plugin compares the two streams, measures the offset and corrects it. The Live screen and browser panel show the stage: Measuring sync, then Sync locked.

Once it is locked

Your microphone's own delay is now known, so the correction keeps tracking the camera's latency by itself — through silence and straight after a reconnect — and recalibrates if you change audio gear mid-stream.

To force a fresh measurement, tap Recalibrate in the sync pill, on the phone or in the browser panel. It's offered only while locked, the one state where it does anything.

ReadoutMeaning
Nothing shownAuto-calibrate isn't running.
Measuring syncKeep talking.

RecalibratingRe-measuring after a change. Not an error.

When your microphone is the slow one

Some USB interfaces add enough latency that the mic arrives after the video, where delaying it further can't help. Auto video delay for slow mics delays the video instead.

A fixed correction, without auto-calibrate

With Auto-calibrate off, the delay checkbox alone shifts your mic by the measured camera latency. A known device delay goes in Audio device's own latency and is subtracted.

Why not just type a number in OBS? Camera latency isn't a constant — it moves with resolution, codec, link and network. A measured offset survives switching from 1080p60 Wi-Fi to 4K30 USB. A typed one doesn't.

Screen-mirror audio

The LensLink Screen source carries real, playable app and system audio — not a timing reference. iOS mutes DRM apps during a broadcast, and your microphone isn't included: screen mirroring.
